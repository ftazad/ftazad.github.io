I build machine learning methods that forecast complex dynamic systems when data are scarce and the rules keep changing.

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at Arizona State University, advised by Prof. K. Selçuk Candan in the EMIT Lab. My research sits at the intersection of artificial intelligence, data systems, and causality: causally-informed learning for large-scale, heterogeneous multivariate time series that are high-dimensional, noisy, sparse, and subject to regime shift.

The problem became concrete for me in May 2020, building SIRTEM against live COVID-19 data. Counts arrived late, were revised without notice, and aggregated away the regional structure that drives transmission. No amount of model capacity fixes that. What was missing was a way to reason about what the data did not resolve, especially at onset, when forecasts matter most and evidence is thinnest.

Before my Ph.D., I spent several years as a faculty member in Bangladesh, most recently as an Assistant Professor at United International University, and I hold an M.B.A. in Finance. I collaborate closely with experts in public health, sustainability, and enterprise systems.

Research

  • Causally-informed representation learning

    A standard factorization machine's interaction term is symmetric, so it cannot represent the direction of a causal edge. I generalize it to an asymmetric low-rank factorization VU⊤ and inject directed, lagged causal structure through interaction masking and Frobenius-norm regularization, and characterize how graph density and directional asymmetry govern when this helps.

    Ongoing work; FMAM and FM-enhanced Crossformer at WiML (NeurIPS 2022, 2025)

  • Knowledge transfer from data-rich to data-poor settings

    A domain disentanglement framework separates location-invariant causal features from location-specific confounders, so shared structure transfers to regions with little data without requiring target supervision. Validated on epidemic onset forecasting across communities.

    Epidemics10, 2025

  • Spatio-causal forecasting and mechanistic models

    Forecasts that ignore geography fail worst at onset. I formalize spatio-causal situation awareness and extend compartmental epidemic models with inter- and intra-region mobility, showing that spatially homogeneous models underestimate severity across connected regions.

    ACM TSAS 2024; SIRTEM, ACM TSAS 2022

  • Counterfactual reasoning with simulation ensembles

    PanCommunity and DataStorm-EM move from prediction to intervention: coupling four simulators so policymakers can compare the trajectories that follow from competing response strategies, with interventions propagating across systems rather than evaluated in isolation.

    arXiv:2407.14571; Epidemics9, 2023

Looking ahead

Next, I am working on causal discovery under scarcity, using LLM-based agents that propose mechanisms from published literature and refine them against statistical discovery on observed streams; time series foundation models pretrained on simulation ensembles so that mechanistic knowledge enters through the pretraining distribution itself; and open systems and benchmarks for causally-constrained streaming forecasting evaluated under distribution shift.
