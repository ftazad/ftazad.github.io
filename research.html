Research

Causally-informed machine learning for forecasting complex dynamic systems under data scarcity and regime shift.

Overview

Why causality, and why now

My research lies at the intersection of artificial intelligence, systems, and causality. I develop machine learning systems with causal understanding for large-scale temporal data subject to regime shifts, spanning three areas: machine learning algorithms that leverage causality, using sparsity as a structural prior on the causal graph to recover mechanisms that remain invariant when the data-generating process changes; data-intensive systems that scale these methods to heterogeneous streaming data; and validation on real epidemic and disaster data.

The problem became concrete for me in May 2020, while building SIRTEM against live COVID-19 data. The state-level dashboards everyone was fitting to reported incompletely and irregularly: counts arrived late, were revised without notice, and aggregated away the regional structure that drives transmission. No amount of model capacity fixes that. What was missing was not a closer fit to the observed curve but a way to reason about the regions the data did not resolve. Forecasting in dynamic complex systems is hard because behavior is emergent, and data scarcity makes it harder precisely during the onset window when forecasts matter most.

Current work

Research thrusts

Spatio-causal framework and formalism

Forecasts that ignore geography transfer a model fitted where data are plentiful to a region whose mobility patterns and risk tolerance differ, and the error is largest exactly at onset. Spatio-causal situation awareness establishes the theoretical foundations and empirical methodology for incorporating spatio-causality into forecasting, targeting prediction accuracy and cross-context knowledge transfer together.

ACM TSAS 2024; SIGSPATIAL 2026 tutorial

Spatially-informed forecasting with mechanistic models

SIRTEM extends the classical compartmental epidemic model to account for inter- and intra-region mobility, showing how spatial context governs propagation dynamics. Models assuming spatial homogeneity underestimate outbreak severity across interconnected regions, and the underestimation is worst during the onset window on which policy decisions depend.

ACM TSAS 2022; Epidemics9, 2023

Alternative timelines within simulation ensembles

PanCommunity and DataStorm-EM move the question from prediction to intervention. They model heterogeneous communities responding differently to epidemics, floods, and hurricanes, and let policymakers compare the trajectories that follow from competing response strategies across four coupled simulators, where an intervention in one propagates into the others.

arXiv:2407.14571; Epidemics9, 2023

Causally-informed representation learning

The standard factorization machine's interaction term is symmetric, so it cannot represent the direction of a causal edge. I generalize it to an asymmetric factorization VU⊤, inject directed lagged causal structure through masking and Frobenius-norm regularization, and characterize how graph density and directional asymmetry govern the error. A domain disentanglement framework then separates location-invariant causal features from location-specific confounders, enabling transfer from data-rich to data-poor regions without target supervision.

Epidemics10, 2025; WiML at NeurIPS 2022, 2025

Looking ahead

Future directions

My five-year agenda develops causally-informed machine learning for spatio-temporal forecasting in coupled complex systems, with applications in public health, sustainability, and critical infrastructure. Three directions define the initial program.

Causal discovery under scarcity

Obtaining a causal graph from limited data is the principal bottleneck. An agentic framework in which LLM-based agents extract candidate mechanisms from published literature and refine them against statistical discovery on observed streams produces structures domain experts can audit.

Foundation models with mechanistic priors

Time series foundation models inherit the correlational structure of their observational pretraining corpora, and fine-tuning cannot remove what pretraining installed. Pretraining on simulation ensembles lets mechanistic knowledge enter through the pretraining distribution itself.

Data-intensive systems and evaluation

Causal regularization, streaming updates, and ensemble simulation impose distinct computational costs whose combination is unstudied, motivating an open framework for causally-constrained streaming forecasting and benchmarks that evaluate under distribution shift rather than on i.i.d. datasets.

Lab and funding plan

The lab's independent contribution lies in machine learning architecture design, causal model components, and efficient algorithms, with interpretability built in at the algorithmic level. Planned funding targets NSF CISE programs including CAREER, Information Integration and Informatics, Smart and Connected Health, Cyber-Physical Systems, and Robust Intelligence.

Selected research projects

  • Jan 2024 – Present
    Causally-Informed Factorization Machine for Multivariate Time Series Forecasting
    • Generalized factorization-machine formulation with lag-augmented inputs and asymmetric low-rank interactions VU⊤ for directed lagged causal effects.
    • Three causal-injection strategies (interaction mask, Frobenius regularization, hybrid) compared against non-causal baselines, with analysis of how graph density and direction asymmetry affect forecasting error.
  • Jan 2022 – Present
    Domain Disentanglement for Transferable Spatio-Temporal Representations
    • Separates domain-invariant from domain-specific structure to support selective knowledge transfer from data-rich to data-poor settings.
    • Validated on epidemic onset forecasting across multiple communities under regime shift.
  • Jan 2022 – 2024
    Simulation Ensembles for Counterfactual Reasoning in Coupled Dynamic Systems
    • Non-monolithic simulation-ensemble framework (PanCommunity / DataStorm-EM) for exploring alternative timelines and intervention analysis.
  • Jan 2022 – May 2024
    Factorization-Machine Attention for Efficient Transformer Forecasting
    • An attention mechanism exploiting second-order feature interactions to reduce self-attention computation in transformer-based forecasting.
  • May 2020 – Dec 2021
    Spatially-Informed Optimization for Epidemic Resource Allocation
    • Single-city and multi-city spatio-temporal forecasting integrated with constrained optimization for allocating limited testing resources.
  • Jun – Aug 2026
    Agentic AI for Causal Discovery from Literature, NSF APPEX Center
    • LLM-based agents that propose, test, and refine candidate causal structures from published literature for downstream predictive hypothesis generation.
